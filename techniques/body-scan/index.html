Body Scan

Ongoing practice — build capacity over time, not just in a crisis.

  1. Sit or lie down
  2. Start with your awareness at your toes
  3. Work your way up your body
  4. Notice sensations and tension
  5. Don't try to fix anything
Why
Shifts attention from anxious thought loops into present-moment bodily sensation, lowering arousal.
Tradition
Core practice in MBSR, Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction.
When
Before bed. After a stressful day.

What it is. The body scan is a structured meditation practice in which attention is systematically moved through different regions of the body, observing physical sensation without trying to change it. It is not relaxation; it is attention training. The quality being developed is the ability to notice what the body is doing without reacting to it, assigning meaning to it, or trying to fix it.

How to do it. 1. Lie down or sit in a supported position. Close the eyes or soften the gaze downward. 2. Begin at the feet. Rest attention on the soles of the feet. Notice whatever is there, temperature, pressure, contact, tingling, nothing. Stay for 15–30 seconds. 3. Move attention slowly upward: feet to ankles, calves, knees, thighs, pelvis, abdomen, chest, hands, arms, shoulders, neck, face, scalp. 4. At each region, simply observe. If there is tension, notice it. If there is discomfort, notice it. If there is nothing, notice that. The instruction is not to relax; it is to observe. 5. When attention wanders (it will), notice that it has wandered and return to the body region being attended to. 6. A full body scan typically runs 20–45 minutes. Abbreviated versions (10–15 minutes, or single-region focused scans) are effective for regular daily practice.

When to use it. As a regular prevention practice, daily or several times a week. Not as a fire extinguisher during acute distress (the attention required is not accessible in high-activation states). Particularly useful for people who tend toward Dissociation or who have difficulty feeling the body, the body scan builds the interoceptive pathway that makes body-based regulation possible. Also used as a preparation for sleep.

Mechanism. The body scan directly trains Interoception, the nervous system's capacity to sense and attend to internal body states. In anxious individuals, interoceptive awareness is often either suppressed (dissociation, numbing) or amplified into threat-scanning (somatic hypervigilance). The body scan builds a third relationship: neutral, curious observation.

Neurologically, consistent body scan practice increases insula thickness and activity, the insula being the primary cortical region processing interoceptive information. It also activates the Parasympathetic Nervous System through slow, attentive, non-alarmed engagement with the body. MBSR (Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction, Jon Kabat-Zinn, University of Massachusetts) placed the body scan at the center of its 8-week protocol, and MBSR is one of the most extensively researched anxiety and pain interventions in clinical literature.

Evidence level: well-studied (within MBSR protocol); clinical use (somatic approaches)
